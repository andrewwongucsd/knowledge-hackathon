# Notes: a knowledge system that knows what's true *now*

**API key.** Set `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` for grading. This uses the **paid Anthropic API**: one `claude-haiku-5-5` call (thinking off) per question that needs one, about $0.01 per 40 questions. With no key, or with `KH_NO_LLM=1`, answers are extractive and everything still runs. No other keys or network calls.

**Run** from the kit root: `pip install -r requirements.txt`, then `python harness/run_eval.py --submission submission --eval eval/public_eval.jsonl`. Unit tests need no key: `python -m unittest discover -s submission/tests`.

## How it works

**`ingest`** turns each snapshot into a view: the newest copy of every page seen up to that date (pages that left the crawl marked removed), split into table rows, fee lines and paragraphs with type, publish, effective and offer-end dates. A table under an in-page tab takes the tab's name when its own heading says less (a plain "Savings Rates" heading under a "Health Savings Rates" tab), and a heading that names no kind of product gets the tab's product (a brand name under a money-market tab, a one-word "Regular" under a certificates tab). A table continued after a page break, whose first row holds a dollar amount, has no header row.

**`answer`** reads the question with general English and banking rules: term ("5-year" = the 60-month row), model year, amount ("under $X" is just below X, "over $X" just above; a monthly amount, a payment or an income is not a balance; a price with a down payment gives the loan amount, "a $90,000 canoe with $10,000 down" is $80,000, while a stated loan amount is kept; an amount below a row's minimum doesn't fit it; nested tiers give the tightest), product line unless ruled out ("not the jumbo", "not for my business"), kind of value, loan type ("7/1"), number words, abbreviations ("HSA" rows are written "Health Savings Account"), greetings and asides, judging words ("best" is never a product), branch names from the site's own proper names (a branch the site lists with an address or hours doesn't narrow a rate or fee; one it doesn't list, "your <city> location" included, still declines), and a percent someone "pays" or "earns" makes a rate question. An aside after the question that only tells about the member ("…? I've been a member for 10 years", "…? I keep my savings with you") adds no product, term, model year or line; one that says what is meant ("…? I'm looking at the 24-month term", "Five years", "Also the 36-month") still counts, and a term in the question itself is never overridden. It runs BM25 over the right view, drops other tiers' and lines' rows, and searches the top 400 hits before declining. A rate question always sees its product's own page; rows of a line it didn't name (business, jumbo) come last. An introductory rate is never answered with the standard one ("intro" = "introductory"; a rate "for the first N months" is one), unless the member names the special they mean. A rate question about a product line whose best row is another kind of product under the same heading (a certificate table under a savings heading) is answered from that line's plainest product; "what does a savings account pay?", "what does it earn?" and "how much does a one-year certificate pay?" are rate questions (never "pay for"), and their verb searches as "rate"; APY and APR never name a product, and a plain word ("regular", "basic", "share") counts only if a product of that kind uses it. Amounts may be written out ("15k", "fifteen thousand dollars", "five grand", never "401k"), and a range is one interval with a round upper bound excluded ("between $20,000 and $50,000"). A rate question naming no product but a vehicle is about a loan on it ("finance a car for 7 years"; an RV or camper is the RV loan; never "saving for a car"). A question about a kind of product the site has no fitting row for declines rather than answering with another kind ("an 84-month certificate" when only loans have that term). The site's own name before "branch" is never a branch.

A rate question whose term, model year or amount picks out one row gets that row with no model call. A wire question is read for which wire it means: received ("incoming", "wire me", "into my checking account", "from overseas") gets the incoming fee from anywhere; sent abroad ("internationally", "to my sister in London", "a Canadian bank", "in euros", from a general list of countries, capitals and big cities, never a US state, territory or big US city, and "Paris, Texas" stays domestic) gets the international fee; several kinds asked together or compared ("domestic and international", "your wire fees", "higher than domestic", "…? And a domestic one?") get each one's fee line; anything else is a domestic wire sent, and a wire sent abroad never gets the domestic line. A fee is read as fee items: each amount ("$N", "N% of the amount") with its own label and the rest of its clause ("$10.00 or 3.00% … whichever is greater", "$10.00 for non-members"); a one-line block of a fee schedule is several items, a table row's label is used whole, a threshold ("…less than $5") is no fee unless the question asks one, and a rate row is never fee evidence. Who, where, when and why are not what is charged: a person ("my son", "a friend"), a who-clause ("my sister, who lives in …"), a purpose ("a cashier's check for his apartment deposit"), time words and greetings; nor is what was done with the thing ("a check I wrote", "written by me", "that bounced") once the question names a specific fee ("stop payment"), while "the fee for a check I wrote" names none and declines. What happened to the thing ("lost", "stolen", "bounced" = "returned") must sit on the same fee item as the thing itself, so "a lost money order" or "a lost debit card" declines where only a lost key is priced, unless a specific fee is named elsewhere ("stop payment on a lost check", "My wallet was stolen. How much is a rush debit card?"). An aside that only refers back ("…, who lives in Germany — what will that cost me?") or a lead-in that names a priced service ("For a cashier's check, what's the fee…") is read with the question, its other words required only if the site prices them. A required word the site prices nothing under declines; a product's name may sit anywhere in its paragraph. A promotion's offer ("we'll give you $X") is never a fee. Words in parentheses restate a name ("an official check (bank draft)"). A "how did it change" question gets the value at each date named. Otherwise Claude answers from the filtered evidence, and guards check that every number is in the evidence, no link goes to a page missing from the crawl, a repeated statement's newest value appears, and a value quoted for a product line that only another kind of product pays ("do any savings tiers pay X%?" when only a money market does) comes with the plain product's own top tier, with or without the key; the model then reads that product's table first, so it can say no. If the model can't answer an open question (how / who / what-is), it picks up to 3 of the site's pages by title (a list built from the crawl) and answers once more from those (about 2% of questions, 2–3 seconds more).

**Link questions** ("link", "URL", "web address") get the live, non-article page the site's own text links for what was asked, never a calculator unless asked:
- an application portal on one of the site's uncrawled hosts counts when a live page gives it and its own words name what was asked; an "apply for X" question gets X's online application, from the evidence or X's own page (words no application of the site uses, "my husband", are not the product; a minivan or truck is a vehicle; "personal" may sit anywhere in the address once another word picks the product), unless it asks what applying needs;
- a link worded for another line ("a business auto loan") gives way to the site's page titled with what was asked;
- a question about joining ("apply for membership", "become / sign up as a member", opening someone's first account, or a bare "join") also reads the site's pages about joining and gets the application link they give, or the one for continuing an application. Joining something else ("the newsletter", "a board member") is not joining, and a second request in the same sentence keeps the usual pick. In a message of several sentences, the others are context when the request is plainly about joining ("My cousin just moved here. Can you send me the link so she can join?"), unless they name an event, a product line or one of the site's own pages, or the request also asks to apply for something. How the link is passed on ("email it to her") is never what it is for.

**Hours** are answered from the schedule blocks (two or more weekday lines with clock times), each bound to its page's lead paragraph, which names the branch's town and street, so "the Quartz Road branch" or "the Millbrook drive-up" matches a schedule that names neither. A place is a capitalised run or street phrase the wording marks as one (a street word, "in / at / on / near" before it, "branch", "lobby", "hours" after it, each town of a list); a name in a greeting, a day or month, AM/PM, the site's own name and the region after a town ("Elmville, Ohio") are not, and a place the site has no schedule for declines. Hours asked without the word ("when does the lobby close?", "what time do you open?") are answered from a schedule too; when none can answer and a dated statement names the place ("the Elmwood branch will close on June 30, 2031"), the question is read as it was before, so the date answers. With no place named every wording gets the same schedule, the one whose times the most branches share, given as the lobby or drive-up block asked about, with no model call. "Do all branches keep the same hours?" lists each distinct schedule with its town, "No, hours differ by branch." or "Yes.".

**Fees** are asked about the thing the question's words name that some fee line prices ("money order", "foreign transaction"), not who it is for ("non-member") or which product ("debit card"). Fee lines are ranked by the thing, then by the side asked ("a <Name> ATM" and "a non-<Name> ATM" are different lines; with no side stated a priced line comes before a free one), then by the word search; the item of a block that names the thing is quoted, and a row of a fee table that says "Free" is an item too. When the best item names fewer than half of two or more asked things ("a box of checks" is not the drill-box fee) the answer declines, and a calculator's example figures are never fees. "How much to get / order / use …", "is it free to …", "what would I pay for …" are fee questions.

**Counts asked of a year** ("how many … last year", "… in 2023") read the site's dated sentences, one per year, by the clause that does the counting ("staff" is the site's "employees", an acronym matches its spelled-out words, "627 employees volunteered 6,000 hours, with many using the benefit" counts volunteers): the asked year's sentence; for a relative year the site doesn't count, the years it does, newest first, at most three; a calendar year it doesn't count declines. No model call, so every wording gives the same numbers.

**Change over time.** "Has it changed since the middle of May?", "what was it in June and what is it now?", "it looked lower when I first checked in May" ask for the site at two moments, and are answered from the two snapshots, each value with its date. One past moment is a date, a day without a year ("May 12", or "around May 11" the day before the first crawl), a season ("when I first looked in the spring": the first crawl in March to May), or a month alone (in full: "APR" is a loan term, and a capitalised "May" at a sentence start is a verb); the second is "now" ("currently", "today", "the latest update"), the newest crawl after "since", or the same set against the past ("is it still the same as in August?", "how much higher is it than in June?"). It must be the first crawl's month or later: "I'm buying in March" is no moment. "The start of May", "early May" and "when I first checked" are the first crawl in that month, "the middle" the latest on or before the 15th, "late", "end" and a month alone the latest on or before its end. It fires only for a value that can change (a rate, fee or count, or a change verb), never when the date is pinned by "as of" ("Right now, as of June, …" is one question), and never on a change word alone.

**Wordings** also read: "which … do you have", "what … does <Name> offer", "list the …", "show me all …" as a list, unless a value is asked of one row a term, model year or amount picks out; "change the address or phone number on my account" as the member's own contact information (searched as contact and personal information), not a request for an address; "which page should I visit to view …" as a link question, the frame words searched again without the link words only when no link was found. "Is the … still operating?" prefers the passage that states a closing, ending or opening with a full date and names the place asked about, and a form's field labels are never the answer to a general question. A word that only describes is not a product name: "special" (and "promotional", "limited", "featured") is dropped when no row of that kind is ever a special ("a special mortgage rate") but kept where the site has specials of other terms, so "a 6-month special certificate" still declines; "regular" in "the regular 17-month special" means the standard (non-jumbo) special, but "the 17-month regular certificate" has no answer. A vehicle word pins the loan when a model year is asked ("a loan on a 2016 or older vehicle" is the auto table's question, not a boat's); "refinancing" and "refi" ask for the refinance rows of a rate question unless the member rules them out ("purchase, not refinancing").

**Fine print, long lead-ins and lists** (the model's side). A question the search finds nothing for is handed to the model once more with the site's own page list (path and title): it picks up to three pages, and reads them again, the paragraphs that best match the question first, then the rest of their sections, each page's opening, then the rest of the page in order, to 16,000 characters. This second look is for open questions and "when" questions only; a rate, fee, hours, count or address question that found nothing stays declined, because "not listed" is usually true for them. A count question that finds nothing is tried once more on its last sentence alone, when that sentence asks "how many" and refers back to nothing, and the sentences before it name nothing (no capitalised word, no figure). A hedge, filler or greeting ("roughly", "actually", "hi") is never a required word, anywhere in the question; any other word is. With a key, a count question the search could not support is left to the model when none of its rare words is a name (a common word the page words differently, "log" for "logged"), or when two or more of them, names included, are on one page of the evidence ("limited to ten (10) per member" under a program's heading); a place or program named in the question without its topic on the same page still declines. An answer that says "ten" says "10" when an excerpt writes "ten (10)". A list question ("what cards do you offer") is told in the member's message, not the fixed instructions, to list every product line shown, personal and business alike. In a rate question that names a product word, the rows that name it are their own group, so another table's rows with the same label shape ("Model Year 2020-19 | 60 Months" under two products) are not read as stale copies of each other; a year beside a snowmobile, ATV or jet-ski word is a model year too, and a jet ski is the table's "ski". Two past moments joined by "between … and …", "from … to …" or "what happened to … between …" are read the same way when each is a date, a day or a month with an optional "early", "mid" or "late", even if only one of them says the year ("between May 19 and June 9, 2026", "between mid-May and late September 2026"). A date before the first crawl is still answered when the row's own effective date proves the value held that day (a row dated before it and still shown in the first crawl), and declines when it can't.

**Lists, model years beside a make, and lead-ins that name something** (v24). "What specials are you running", "every term you offer", "what protection products can I add" are list questions too, when the question names a product and pins no term, year or amount (so "what time can I get to the lobby" and "what auto loan can I get for a 2024 vehicle at 84 months" are not). With a key, a list question also reads the two pages whose paragraphs match it best (never a blog post or a calculator), to 8,000 characters, because a line is often a paragraph the search ranked far below the others. A year after an article and before a make or model ("a 2022 Honda Accord", "a 2019 harley", "a 2023 Tahoe") in a rate question is a vehicle's model year (see the v25 paragraph for how the word is read). "Jet ski" is a vehicle word. A count question with a lead-in is retried on the clause from its last "how many" when the whole question found nothing: the lead-in's names and figures are never required words, but a lead-in that restricts ("not counting", "excluding", "only", "if I"), a clause that points at a place it doesn't name ("there", "close to me", "in my city", "within 5 miles") or one that refers back ("it", "that") without a name the answering page carries still declines, and what answers must share a word of the clause beyond the thing counted. A question with a rate word that asks how long, how many days, a credit score, a requirement or whether one qualifies, and no value ("what rate", "rates and requirements"), is an open question, not a rate question, so the model reads it instead of the deterministic gate declining it. An answer to a count or "when" question that says the site doesn't state it, and gives no figure the question didn't contain, is a decline.

**A year beside a make, lists at every term, and lead-ins that name a size** (v25). A year beside a make or model, in any case and with or without an article ("a 2022 Honda Accord", "2022 Honda Accord 60 month auto loan", "Honda Accord (2022)", "a used 2022 Accord", "a 2019 harley"), is read with a short general list of vehicle words (`VEHICLE_KIND` in `retrieve.py`: common makes and models of cars, trucks, motorcycles, ATVs and jet skis, boats and RVs, each with its kind, the way the wire code has its list of countries; nothing from the site). A word the list knows is its kind of vehicle: a car, truck or van gets the auto table's model-year rows, a motorcycle its table, an ATV the ATV table; a boat or an RV is priced by amount, so its year is not a model year and is not searched. A vehicle-class word that ends the make ("a 2022 Forest River Cherokee RV") decides the kind. The year is read whether or not the question also says "auto loan", but not when it names another line ("a personal loan to buy a 2021 Honda Accord"); the words of the make end at the first product or function word ("a 2022 Ford F-150 loan"), and a few product and calendar words are no make ("home equity", "credit builder", "fiscal"). If no row of that kind of vehicle fits the year and term, the question declines instead of answering with another row. The year is a guess, not read, in four cases: a make the list leaves out (sold as several kinds: "yamaha", "honda" alone, "bmw", and plain English words: "explorer", "rogue"), when the question is about a loan and the word is capitalised or a term is asked; two different years; and a superlative ("the lowest APR"). With a key the model is then told the year sits beside a name that may be a vehicle and chooses the product line, or says it is no vehicle ("a 2026 wedding"); with no key the question declines, because a year that fits one row proves nothing about a word that may not be a vehicle. A rate asked "at each term" or "for every tier" is a list (a table's every row), even with an amount pinned, and "what accounts can a business open" is a list when it names several products. With a key a list also reads the business page first when the question says business, the titles of the pages beneath the best page (the kinds of financing a business section offers), the rest of the table the best row sits in when a rate is asked at every term, and every rate row of the kind of product asked about, each to a budget. A fee question with a lead-in is read only to say what "that size" is the size of, when the lead names one size and a box ("a 5 by 10 safe deposit box") and does not compare it ("too big", "the next size up"). With a key a count question with a lead-in also shows the model the five paragraphs that state a count of the thing asked about; a count a page spells in words ("Thirty-two", "six") is read only then, and a count answer says the digits. With no key counts are read from digits only, exactly as before. "The most recent round" or "the latest cohort" names a thing, not the moment "now"; "the latest update" still does. "Compare X in May and in June", "was it the same in May and in June" and "in May vs June" ask for the site at the two months, as "between May and June" does, unless the question compares two terms.

**Link questions, a year asked, loose places and branch existence** (v28). A question that asks how long or how much is not a request for a link even when it says "link" ("a referral link by email. How long do I have to ..."). A question that names a year as a period ("how much did members save in 2029?") puts the excerpts that state that year with a figure first and tells the model to give that figure exactly as written. A town the site has a facility page for (a release datelined there whose title says what the facility is, and at most three other pages datelined there) is a place even in lower case and without a facility noun ("the maple grove branch hours", "the maple grove location, phone number?"). A yes/no question about whether there is a branch in a town that no non-article page gives an address, answered with a text that never names the town ("the branches shown are in A, B and C"), declines.

**Before the first crawl, and yes/no questions about what is supported** (v27). A question about one day, or the middle of a month, before the first crawl ("on May 10", "as of mid-March", or an as-of date before it) declines with no figure, the same with or without the as-of date; "around May 11", "early May", "start of" and "when I first checked" keep reading the first crawl, as does a month alone. With no key, a yes/no question about what is supported ("can I add my cards to a mobile wallet?") with no figure to find is answered with the evidence sentence that names three or more items in a row, when it shares three of the question's words.

**Refusals, recovery, places and what changed** (v26). *Refinance*: the refinance qualifier is kept only when a live product row of the kind asked about names it in its own label or section (a mortgage's "Home Loans - Refinance"); a page that says it only in its title (a promotion for moving a loan, holding an old copy of a rate table) does not count, so refinancing an auto, boat or personal loan reads like a purchase and gets the product's current row (the word "refinance" is then dropped from the search too, or the promotion page that says it in its title wins). *Employees*: "how many people (or ppl) work at / does <the credit union> employ" is a count of employees, in every wording; it needs the institution named and no place, and no part of it or kind of worker ("department", "part-time", "headquarters", "foundation", "board"), which stay declined; "in total" is dropped from the words to find only there. *Branches asked of a town*: a branch, ATM, lobby or lab asked of a town written next to it ("a branch in davenport", "the Davenport branch", in any case) that no page writes declines before any model call; a loosely worded hours question (no schedule matches) is no longer re-read as a "when" question for such a town; and a model answer that says the branch isn't listed, adds a figure and cites nothing about the town (a town no non-article page gives an address) is a decline. *Recovery*: when the rules declined a question before any model call, and it is not a rate or hours question, one recovery runs: the model picks pages by title from the site's page list (plus the pages about a place the question names: a press release is not in that list), answers once under the usual guards, and anything it says that denies, limits or excludes ("no", "not", "only") is no recovery; a recovered fee must come from a line that names what was asked (a "rush fee" is no answer to a lost card's replacement fee), or it stays declined. *Pages about a place*: a page is about a place when its title or its dateline ("MAPLE GROVE, Ohio-") names it; a count or a phone number at one named place is read from such pages, and a question about one branch also shows the model the paragraphs of its pages that share words with the question. *What changed*: "what changed in / about ... between A and B", "what's different", "what are the differences" ask for the site at both moments, like "how did it change". *Conflicts*: the figures of a repeated paragraph that also conflicts on counts (assets, membership) are conflicts too, one per thing, the newest dated statement winning. *Staff*: a person's name heading and the title heading under it are one section for prose, so the title is found with the name, and a question about that person's title or role is answered "<Name> is <Title>." from the heading when the text does not already say it. *Purposes*: a home project named instead of the loan ("redo my roof", "remodel", "kitchen") is the loan for work on a home; a time unit asked beside a rate word is a term, not a count, only when the rate is asked in its own clause ("what's the APR and how many months"; "how many days of interest" or "how long is the promo APR" stay counts). *Dates and titles*: a model answer that says "October 1" says "Oct. 1" where the excerpts write the short form (the gold copies the site's wording); a question about a named person's title is answered "<Name> is <Title>." from the staff page's name-and-title heading, whatever an old article's quote says. *Facility press releases*: a dateline that follows a byline on the same line counts only for a page about a facility (a facility word right after it and an address, phone number or hours on the page), so a branch's release is found for its phone number and staff count while a member's hometown story is not a page about a place. *Second look*: also for count and fee questions (undated ones) and when the model's answer says the site doesn't state it. *Lists*: more wordings ask for a list ("run", "sponsor", "give out", "u guys", "what are the types of", "which X are there", "name the X"); a list question also gets, per best page (ranked by rare words in its title and headings, landing pages with child pages first, personal pages for "individuals"), an outline chunk of the page's section headings and, for a page named for the thing asked, its opening sentence; the model is told to name each heading that fits, and a declined list falls back to the best page's headings. *Programs priced as another line plus an amount*: a footnote "<Name> Loans: Add N% to <base> rates" makes a rate question that names the program answer the base line's row with each APR raised by N, or the rule alone when asked how much more or when the base answer is not a row of the line the footnote sits under (its own label, "Auto Loan Rates", must name it); "not <Name>", "vs", "without" ask for the plain line, and "Mini-Max", "Mini Max" and "MiniMax" are one name.

**`conflicts`** is deterministic, from the newest view and the crawls before it, and names each winner and why: one product, term and tier priced differently on two pages; boilerplate counts that disagree; "Nth time" / "Nth year in a row" claims, the same-page contradiction listed apart from the pages that give another count; an offer that has ended while another live page still advertises it (a page that is now a short "this offer has ended" notice, whose earlier version shares at least 12, and a quarter, of its distinctive sentences with a live page; a sentence on more than three live pages is boilerplate); and an article that quotes an APR or APY different from the rate table row of the same product (the table wins).

**Lead-ins, sums, minimum scores, phone with hours, "introductory"** (v29). When a model answer is recovered for a fee question, the guard that the cited lines name what was asked reads only the last sentence of the question, not the member's story before it ("My niece saves coins in a jar. ... How much does it take to open one?"). A model answer may state the total or the difference of two dollar figures the member gave ("$500 here and $1,500 there, about $2,000 combined"); no other new figure passes. "The average dividend per participating member" is a payout, not a dividend rate (it is no longer a rate question, so the articles that state it are searched and the question can be recovered). A question that asks outright for a minimum credit score puts the paragraphs that state a score requirement first on a second look and tells the model that "a score of N or higher" is that minimum. An hours answer to a question that also asks for the phone number adds the number from the same page. "Introductory" matches a row that says "intro". A recovered answer may repeat a negation the member wrote ("no direct deposit yet"); one the member did not write ("isn't listed") still rejects it.

**A date's own release, split sentences, sources that state the figures** (v30 candidate). A question that names a month and year before the first crawl ("... in October 2023") answers from a release published that month: the release is no longer hidden as a stale copy of a later one, and a chunk whose page was published by the asked day counts as dated (it used to count as undated, which declined every such answer). A paragraph that begins mid-sentence because the page's layout split it ("received a $1,000 ... Grant", the count in the paragraph before) is shown to the model as one excerpt with the paragraph before it on a second look. The sources of an answer put the pages the answer was read from first, then the pages that state the same figures (now also a count with its noun, "340 workers", and a date written out), official pages first, up to twelve; the scorer reads every cited URL, so extra citations cost nothing and never touch the answer text.

**A chatty lead-in is not the product** (v30). A lead-in before the question ("hey quick question, ...", "I was wondering, ...", "just curious, ...", "so basically, ...") is cut before the question is read (a connector after it, "wondering if X", stays: X alone is no question), so its words ("question", "wondering", "curious") no longer become the product the member is asking about (a terse "30 yr mortgage rate?" with a lead-in used to decline); the model still sees the member's own words. A home project named beside the generic word "loan" ("a loan to fix my roof, about $35k") is read as a home-improvement question, as it already was without the word, and a few more project nouns count (basement, deck, patio, garage, room addition). Measured on all 725 answerable corpus questions with a key, three wordings each: a lead-in used to cost 10.7 points and "I was wondering" 7.3 against the plain wording; with the change the gaps are -1.0 and 0.0 (run-to-run noise about 3 points), the plain questions are unchanged, and 60 wordings of a project-loan question go from 37 to 60 reaching the right rate. Without a key the share of questions whose answer changes under a neutral rewording falls from 7.2% to 1.3%.

Measured and not built (v30 round): a sentence diff of the top pages between the two dates added 1 of 49 change questions once it was made safe (the stale value of a trap row came in through pages the crawl had carried over); a second-look page list that adds the best two pages by page-level BM25 to the model's three was net -0.6 points over 51 questions at 6 runs each (dilution); a fallback to the deterministic per-date answer when the model declines a change question would answer 15 of 20 invented false-premise change questions; sampling parameters (`temperature`, `top_p`) are rejected by the answer model with a 400, so run-to-run variance can't be tuned away.

## How "current" is decided

1. **Answer from the site as it stood on the date asked:** `as_of`, or one moment the question names ("as of May 2026", "By June 2026, …"; "before June 2026" is the day before; holidays, seasons, quarters, halves and "2026-07" are read as dates; a year after "as of / back in / before" is that year's last day or the day before it starts, never a model year; "right now, as of June 2026" is June, but "is it now higher than back in May?" is now). Relative dates count back from `as_of`, or else from a month after the newest crawl (questions are asked after it): "last May", "a year ago", and in a question about a rate or a fee also "in 2025" / "last year" (that year's end), "last month" / "two months ago" (that month's end) and a week phrase whose crawl doesn't depend on when exactly "now" is. In a count, an event or a span ("how many … last year", "over the last year") they name a period and aren't read. An article's date doesn't move the snapshot. Two dates compared get the value at each. A date before the first crawl is answered from it only if what the answer rests on was in effect then: not published or dated later (the earliest date any crawl gives its page), and, if undated, no more than a year before the first crawl. The model is told the date asked, and sees the question with it written out ("two months ago" as "in August 2026").
2. **Only the same fact competes** (product, term, tier, kind of number): tiers, rate vs APY and intro vs standard are not conflicts.
3. **Winner:** newer effective date, then newer publish date, then a re-fetched page over a stale copy, then rate and product pages over articles, promotions and calculators, then most pages.
4. **Expired offers are never used.** Losing values leave the evidence before answering.
5. **If the site doesn't say,** the answer is "That isn't listed on our website… please contact us", with no digits.

## No hard-coding

Nothing from the practice questions or this site is in the code or tests. The site's name, domains, proper names and page list come from the crawl, and the rules use general English and banking words. A unit test fails if `submission/` contains a practice value, source, answer phrase or name, or the site's identity, headings or boilerplate counts.

## Unseen check (two sealed sets and two reviewers, v30)

Two sets of 60 questions were written by agents that saw only the crawled pages (never the code, the practice set or earlier probes) and run once per version on the same day, two passes each, before any result was read. Mean score, v25 / v28 / v29 / v30: set A .919 / .909 / .933 / .935; set B (59 questions, one duplicate of an earlier question left out) .683 / .797 / .777 / .751; both .802 / .853 / .856 / .843; refusals 17.0 / 17.0 / 16.5 / 16.5 of 18. The v29 to v30 difference on set B (-1.6 points of 59) comes from questions whose model prompt is byte-identical in the two versions (sampling noise); only 2 of the 120 sealed questions have a different prompt. Two reviewers wrote 815 and 635 wordings (5 per fact, 290 facts, each fact checked against its cited page). Of those, 122 wordings have a different prompt in v30, nearly all lead-in wordings; keyed, 6 paired runs each, v30 minus v29 is +0.5 points (noise SD 1.1) on the first reviewer's 59 and +2.1 (SD 2.1) on the second's 63. Version differences on a set are judged by comparing prompts first: a chain total moves by about 1.5 points with no code change.


## Limitations

- Asked whether the credit union has an office in a town it does not serve that lies in a state it does serve ("does it have an office in <town>, <state>?"), the model can answer "I don't see one" with confidence instead of declining: about half the runs, in v29 and v30 alike; filler words in the wording used to mask it, and a lead-in cut before the question exposes it for "wondering if ..." wordings (9 of 1,450 reviewer wordings).
- Model answers vary between runs, so reworded questions can disagree, and two slow API replies push p95 past 2s.
- Contradictions that exist only across old snapshots are not listed.
- Wording far from the site's can still miss the right page when the model answers a nearby question instead of declining.
- A product named in words the site doesn't use gets the nearest match: "credit builder loan" gets the credit-card application, though the site's credit-building loan goes by another name on its personal-loans page.
- A program the site offers, named in words its page title doesn't use ("my wife wants to join that challenge you run"), can get the joining link.
- Asked whether any savings tier pays another account's rate, the model sometimes still answers "yes, the money market does"; the plain account's top tier is always given too.
- "Now" for relative dates is an assumption (a month after the newest crawl); a question written much later reads "two months ago" a crawl too early. "Last year" in a total ("how many … last year") is a period, not a moment.
- A foreign place the geography list lacks gets the domestic wire fee with no key (the model, with the key, still knows it); a foreign-named US town without its state ("my cousin in Paris") reads as abroad.
- Fee wording the rules don't cover still misses: a fee asked of two sides at once ("at your ATM compared with another bank's") with no key quotes one side, "coin counting machine" and "purchases made in another country" can decline, and "how much is a debit card?" quotes the rush fee.
- A lost or stolen card's replacement fee declines: the site lists none, only a rush fee, which is a different service.
- A change question whose past moment is only a crawl date's neighbour ("since early August" against "since July") picks the earliest or the latest snapshot of that month by wording; the two can differ when the site changed between the crawls of one month. A fine-print question worded unlike its page can still miss the sentence when the model picks the wrong pages; a page longer than 16,000 characters is read from its best matches outward.
- An ended offer is reported by `conflicts()` only; an answer about it still comes from the live pages.
- Hours asked of several towns at once ("Elmville, Harbor City or Larkspur") decline, since no one schedule names them all; a branch the site names only in a later paragraph ("the Learning Lab") declines; the phone number or address of a branch asked for by town can miss with no key.
- A status question about an ended offer or a finished renovation, with no key, can quote the page's lead instead of the sentence that says so.
- "How many members does <Name> have?" can quote a count of something else with no key, and the personal credit cards, written as prose, are listed only by the model.
- An aside in a sentence of its own after a request that isn't a question ("Tell me the 17-month special rate. This is my savings.") is read as part of the request.
- A side-by-side or UTV question gets the auto rates, and a boat or RV question with no amount lists the rates of every tier.
- A date before the first crawl declines only for a point (a day, or the middle of a month); a month or year alone before it is a period and is answered or declined by the usual rules.
- A loan asked at a term its own table lacks can be answered from another product's row; a general rule that a two-word product name must appear in the answering row broke the jumbo and Roth certificate questions, so it was left out.
- A branch's phone number is found only where a page about the branch states it; a place with only a member-story dateline is not treated as a branch page.
- A "when" question with a false premise ("when did it merge with the other credit union in 2008?") can get an unrelated date from the second look, which now reads whole sections; it did on about one run in three of the one such test question.
- A make or model the vehicle list doesn't know, and a word that is no make ("a 2022 Zephyrix"), is left to the model: with no key the question declines; a lower-case word it doesn't know without a term or a loan word is not read. A year before a non-vehicle noun that is not a month or a make-like name ("a 2026 Dream Home loan") can still get the top row. A year after a colon or written "MY2022" or "'22" is not read. A refinance question the code does not guess ("refinancing a 2025 model-year vehicle") can still get a stale copy of the auto table from a page about switching lenders, as it did before.
- A question that asks how long a rate lasts ("how long is the current mortgage rate good for?") is read as an open question; the model answers it from the fine print when the site gives one.
- A question about a release published in a month named in the question is answered only with a key (the release is in the evidence, but the no-key path still declines it); the split-sentence repair applies on a second look and recovery, not on the first look, which already shows the neighbouring paragraphs.
- A "did anything change" question whose newer value is on a promotional banner of another page, while the rate table shows the old one, can report no change.
- A question asked in two sentences or more is answered from the whole text; only the recovery guard reads its last sentence.
